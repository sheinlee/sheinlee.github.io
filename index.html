About Me

Hello! I'm Shicheng, a computational chemist working at the interface of molecular simulation and machine learning. I recently completed my Ph.D. in Chemical Engineering at Vanderbilt University with Prof. De-en Jiang, where my dissertation studied lanthanide coordination chemistry for rare-earth separation — from structure to thermodynamics.

My research combines ab initio molecular dynamics, polarizable force fields, and data-driven and generative modeling to understand how lanthanide ions bind their ligands, and to turn that understanding into more selective separation chemistry. Longer term, I want to build physics-grounded, AI-accelerated design pipelines for selective ligands and separation materials that support sustainable critical-mineral and f-element technologies.

I'm currently looking for opportunities in computational chemistry and molecular machine learning. Feel free to reach out at shicheng.li@vanderbilt.edu.

Research

Mining lanthanide coordination chemistry from the CSD

The Cambridge Structural Database holds tens of thousands of lanthanide single-crystal structures. I built a cheminformatics pipeline to analyze their coordination shells, donor types, and ligand types from the perspective of rare-earth element separations — both to extract chemical design rules and to create a training ground for machine learning and generative models.

Scientific Reports (2024)

Nitrate and water in the first coordination shell

During solvent-extraction separations, water and inorganic anions such as nitrate get pulled into the first coordination shell alongside the organic ligand. This work maps how they compete across the lanthanide series: nitrate prefers bidentate binding and pushes coordination numbers up, while water fills in singly or doubly and keeps the shell flexible.

Inorganic Chemistry Communications (2025)

Polarizable force fields for Ln(III)–ligand binding

Fixed-charge force fields struggle with the strong polarization around trivalent lanthanide ions. I parameterized the AMOEBA polarizable force field for the La–TEDGA system and computed binding free energies in water using umbrella sampling and free-energy perturbation, bridging quantum-level accuracy and simulation timescales that reach real separation chemistry.

Manuscript in preparation.

Generative modeling for ligand design

Starting from my undergraduate thesis at Zhejiang University — a multifunctional molecular generation package for ligand virtual screening — I have continued to work on deep generative chemistry, including E(3)-equivariant graph neural networks and 3D diffusion models aimed at proposing selective ligands for rare-earth separations.
